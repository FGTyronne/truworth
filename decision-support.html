How it works

What sits behind the score and the vote.

TruWorth turns your assumptions into a consistent reality check. It gives you something concrete to challenge before you spend.

Last updated 7 October 2026

1. The TruWorth score

The core score looks at price, ongoing cost, expected use, lasting enjoyment, time saved and the effort or hassle involved. A higher score means the value case is stronger on the inputs you gave us. It is not a universal measure of whether a product is “good”.

2. Different currencies

Money amounts need to behave sensibly across supported currencies. TruWorth therefore uses an internal currency reference when applying monetary parts of the scoring formula, while keeping the price you entered in its original currency. This is a scoring normalisation, not an exchange-rate quote and not an instruction to convert a retailer price.

If a seller price and your financial profile use different currencies, financial-fit guidance and the TruWorth+ purchase vote pause until you confirm the basis. TruWorth does not silently relabel one currency as another.

3. The stress test

The stress test deliberately makes your assumptions tougher, including lower expected use and lower enjoyment. The point is simple: does the value case still look solid when reality is less generous?

4. Financial fit

If you choose to add financial context, TruWorth can compare a purchase with the monthly free cash implied by the income, essential outgoings, debt commitments and savings target you entered. That is a personal comfort signal, not a lender affordability test or credit assessment.

5. Wants You vs Real Buyer You

As your history grows, TruWorth compares what attracts you with what you actually buy and later report using. That can surface patterns such as browsing for enjoyment but buying for utility, becoming more selective, or repeatedly overestimating use in a category.

These patterns change with your behaviour. They are not diagnoses or permanent personality labels.

6. TruWorth+ purchase vote

The score and the vote are deliberately different.Free helps you assess the value case. TruWorth+ can give an eligible subscriber a BUY or DON’T BUY vote.

The premium vote can combine the core assessment, stress-test resilience, optional financial fit and relevant patterns in your TruWorth history. A quick Snap & Assess vote may have less information than a full assessment, so its confidence can be lower.

7. What TruWorth cannot know

TruWorth does not know about anything you have not entered, such as an upcoming bill, income uncertainty, dependants, borrowing terms, emergencies or a change in priorities. It also cannot predict whether a product will actually be as useful or enjoyable as you expect.

8. Product identification

Snap & Assess prioritises barcodes, then on-device text recognition and structured product catalogues. When confidence is poor, TruWorth should ask you to confirm or type the product rather than invent a match. Always check the exact product, variant, price and currency before scoring.

9. You make the final call

A score is evidence to think with. A TruWorth+ vote is personalised decision support. Neither replaces your judgement or professional advice where that is appropriate.
