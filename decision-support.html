How TruWorth works

From “which product is better?” to “which decision is better for you?”

TruWorth separates product assessment from personalised purchase recommendation. The score is deterministic: AI may help explain information in future, but it does not get to invent the mathematical result.

Methodology overview · updated 14 October 2026

1. Two layers, not one

Product assessmentA 0–100 assessment based on the purchase price, lifetime cost, realistic use, lasting enjoyment, ownership friction, time saving, motive and other entered assumptions.
Personal decision recommendationFor eligible TruWorth+ users, the engine can combine the assessment with the user's financial and behavioural profile to produce BUY, WAIT, SKIP or NEITHER.

A product can be objectively attractive yet still be the wrong purchase for a particular person at a particular time.

2. The TruWorth framework

TW = wAA + wUU + wGG + wLL + wVV + wSS + wQQ − wRRThe public framework describes the factors. Protected coefficients, calibration values and thresholds are kept server-side.
A · AffordabilityHow the purchase fits the financial context you entered.
U · UtilityPractical usefulness, realistic frequency of use and meaningful time benefit.
G · Goal alignmentHow well the purchase supports the priorities and goals you say matter.
L · Lifestyle fitWhether the product fits how you actually live and use similar products.
V · ValuePrice relative to realistic lifetime use and ownership cost.
S · SatisfactionExpected lasting enjoyment after novelty fades.
Q · Quality / longevityThe role of durability, useful life and your preference for long-term quality.
R · Purchase riskFactors such as impulse, avoidable duplication, status pressure and behavioural regret risk.

3. Decision Discipline 1–10

Decision Discipline is a user-controlled setting that changes the balance and purchase threshold rather than merely changing the wording shown after a result.

1–3 · Lifestyle-ledGreater emphasis on utility, enjoyment and lifestyle fit, while still surfacing meaningful affordability problems.
4–7 · BalancedBalances enjoyment, value, financial fit, goals and risk.
8–10 · Challenge mePlaces more weight on affordability, value, goal alignment and purchase risk, making WAIT, SKIP or NEITHER more likely where spending is weakly justified.

4. Why the same product can receive a different recommendation

Two users can assess the same phone, watch or laptop and receive different personal recommendations because their free cash, goals, existing alternatives, upgrade behaviour, expected use and Decision Discipline may differ. A user's own recommendation can also change when price or circumstances change.

5. Recommendation outcomes

TruWorth is deliberately not designed to find a reason to buy something. Depending on the evidence, the engine may output:

  • BUY — the purchase clears the current personal threshold.
  • WAIT — there is a case for the product, but not enough to justify buying now.
  • SKIP — the purchase does not currently earn its place.
  • NEITHER — when comparing options, neither is a sufficiently good decision.

6. Confidence is not the score

Recommendation confidence is a separate concept. It can be reduced when information is missing, the price or variant is uncertain, the profile is incomplete, currencies do not match, or two products are too close to distinguish confidently.

7. Price integrity

TruWorth attempts to distinguish exact prices from starting prices, finance amounts, trade-in claims and other promotional figures. If a reliable cash price cannot be established, the app should ask for confirmation rather than silently inventing a value. A missing or invalid price can block a formal financial recommendation.

8. Personal financial context

The current recommendation profile can use income, essential outgoings, debt commitments and a savings target entered by the user. This is not a credit check and does not determine eligibility for credit. TruWorth does not currently need online-banking credentials to calculate a consumer-product recommendation.

9. Behavioural profile and learning

Where enabled, the profile can include impulse tendency, buyer's remorse, upgrade frequency, status sensitivity, preference for quality and goal focus. Purchase follow-ups may help TruWorth compare what a user expected with what actually happened. The long-term goal is to make future estimates more personal and evidence-based.

10. Partner independence

Commercial relationships are excluded from the recommendation logic.Partner discounts, affiliate commission, sponsorship, featured placement or other commercial compensation do not increase the TruWorth score and cannot cause a BUY recommendation.

Member Benefits are shown as a separate value layer after the purchasing decision. The sequence is: decide first, benefit second.

11. Profiling and your control

Personalised recommendations use information from your profile to evaluate the purchase. You can edit your profile, change Decision Discipline, withdraw consent for personalised recommendation use, or choose not to complete the profile. A personalised recommendation is not required to use the basic comparison tools.

12. What TruWorth cannot know

The engine cannot automatically know every upcoming bill, household responsibility, emergency, change in income, emotional circumstance or future preference. It also cannot guarantee future product satisfaction. The quality of the result therefore depends on the quality and completeness of the information supplied.

13. Regulatory boundary

The current product is designed for ordinary consumer-purchase decisions. It is not intended to recommend investments, securities, pensions, regulated credit products, insurance policies or other regulated financial products. Those categories require separate treatment and are outside the current TruWorth purchase recommendation service.

14. Versioning and auditability

TruWorth may update its scoring and recommendation methodology as it is tested and calibrated. Saved assessments carry scoring information so historical results can be understood in context. Material methodology changes should be versioned rather than silently rewriting past decisions.

15. You make the final decision

TruWorth is a decision-support tool. Its purpose is to make trade-offs visible and challenge weak assumptions before you spend. You remain responsible for the final purchase decision.
