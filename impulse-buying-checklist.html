Before you spend

Impulse buying checklist

An unplanned purchase is not automatically a bad purchase. The useful question is whether you still want it once urgency, novelty and the checkout moment are removed.

1. Was I already looking for this?

If the purchase solves a problem you had before seeing the product, that is different from discovering the product first and creating a reason to own it afterwards.

2. Would I buy it at the normal price?

A discount can make spending feel like saving. Ignore the percentage-off label for a moment and ask whether the product itself would still deserve your money.

3. How many times will I actually use it?

Put a number on it. Ten uses, fifty uses and five hundred uses create very different economics. If you cannot picture when and where you will use it, lower your estimate.

4. Do I already own something that does most of this?

Upgrades and duplicates often feel more transformative before purchase than after it. Compare the new product with what you already own, not with having nothing.

5. What will it cost beyond today?

Include accessories, consumables, maintenance or other predictable costs. A low purchase price can hide a high ownership cost.

6. Would waiting 24 hours materially hurt me?

If not, wait. A genuine need will usually still be a genuine need tomorrow. The pause is particularly useful when the main reason to act now is a countdown, limited-stock message or social pressure.

7. What am I giving up instead?

Every purchase uses money that could have stayed available for something else. You do not need to turn every decision into a financial model, but you should know what the trade-off is.

Quick rule: if you cannot explain the expected use, total cost and reason for buying in a few sentences, you probably do not need to decide immediately.
