How TruWorth works

How your purchase score is worked out.

TruWorth uses the information you enter to give a product purchase a consistent 0–100 score and show the main reasons behind it.

Last updated 9 October 2026

1. What TruWorth currently assesses

TruWorth currently assesses physical consumer products: things such as headphones, phones, trainers, bags, skincare, home appliances, fitness equipment and gaming products.

2. What goes into the score

The score uses the purchase price, any extra lifetime costs, how often you expect to use the product, how much lasting enjoyment you expect, the time and effort involved in owning it, and any genuine time it may save you.

TruWorth also asks why you want the purchase. A practical need, something you expect to enjoy for a long time, and a short-lived impulse are not treated as identical decisions.

3. What a higher score means

A higher score means the purchase looks better on the information you supplied. Frequent use, lasting value and a sensible cost per use can strengthen the score. High cost, low expected use, significant upkeep or an impulse-led reason can reduce it.

The score is about the fit between the purchase and your own answers. It is not a product review and it does not mean one brand or product is objectively better than another.

4. Price and currency

TruWorth keeps the price and currency you enter with the assessment. Where monetary values need to be compared internally, the app normalises them so the same scoring approach can be used across supported currencies.

If information needed for a sensible comparison uses different currencies, TruWorth should ask for confirmation rather than silently treating the amounts as the same currency.

5. Optional financial context

If you choose to add financial information, TruWorth can place a purchase alongside the income, essential outgoings, debt commitments and savings target you have entered.

This helps show whether a purchase appears comfortable relative to the information you provided. It is not a credit check, lending decision or formal affordability assessment.

6. Your purchase history

When you are signed in, your saved assessments and purchase tracking can help TruWorth show patterns over time: what you consider, what you actually buy and, where you provide follow-up information, how reality compares with what you expected.

Those patterns are based only on your TruWorth activity. They can change as your behaviour changes.

7. TruWorth+ guidance

The score and the recommendation are different things.The score measures the purchase using your assessment answers. TruWorth+ can use additional account context to give clearer purchase guidance where enough information is available.

That guidance can take account of the assessment, optional financial context and relevant patterns in your saved TruWorth history. When there is not enough information for a reliable conclusion, the app should say so rather than invent certainty.

8. What TruWorth cannot know

TruWorth only knows what you provide. It cannot automatically know about an upcoming bill, a change in income, family commitments, borrowing terms, an emergency expense or a change in your priorities.

It also cannot guarantee that you will use or enjoy a purchase as much as you expect. That is why the assumptions you enter matter.

9. You make the final decision

TruWorth is a decision-support tool. Its job is to make the trade-offs easier to see before you spend. The final purchase decision remains yours, and professional financial, legal or other advice may be appropriate for decisions that require it.
