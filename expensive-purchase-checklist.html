Expensive purchase checklist

For a large purchase, the question is not only whether the money is available. Check whether the product will deliver enough use or enjoyment to justify what you are giving up.

1. Define what “expensive” means for you

A £500 purchase does not have the same financial impact for every person. Consider the amount against your own disposable income, savings goals and other commitments rather than using somebody else’s threshold.

2. Compare the product with a realistic alternative

Do not compare the item only with buying nothing. Compare it with the product you would genuinely buy instead. If a £900 option and a £500 option solve the same problem for you, the additional £400 needs a reason.

3. Estimate lifetime use

Think in months or years where appropriate. A product that lasts longer or gets used daily can justify a higher price than something used occasionally. Then calculate the expected cost per use.

4. Add ownership costs

Include predictable maintenance, accessories, consumables and replacement parts. If those costs are material, they belong in the decision before you buy rather than after.

5. Test the purchase against a bad month

Ask whether buying it would leave you uncomfortable if an ordinary unexpected expense arrived soon afterwards. TruWorth is not a formal affordability assessment, but the question can expose when a purchase would leave too little room.

6. Decide what would make you regret it

Write down the failure case before purchasing: perhaps you use it less than expected, the cheaper option would have been enough, or the product becomes inconvenient to maintain. If that outcome seems likely, change the decision or lower the price you are willing to pay.

7. Give yourself a decision date

For non-urgent expensive purchases, choose a specific date to decide rather than endlessly browsing. The delay creates room for the initial excitement to settle while avoiding weeks of indecision.
