Decision support

What sits behind the score and the vote.

TruWorth is designed to make a purchase decision more explicit, not to pretend a formula knows your life better than you do.

Last updated 7 October 2026

1. The core TruWorth score

The core score looks at the cost of the purchase together with expected use, lasting enjoyment, time saved, ongoing cost and the time or hassle the purchase may add. The score is intended to create a consistent reality check, not an objective measure of whether something is “good”.

2. The stress test

TruWorth deliberately challenges optimistic assumptions. A result may be recalculated with lower expected use and lower enjoyment to show whether the purchase still looks reasonable when reality is less favourable.

3. Financial fit

If you choose to add private financial context, TruWorth can compare a purchase with the monthly free cash implied by the income, essential outgoings, debt commitments and savings target you entered. This is a personal comfort signal, not a creditworthiness or lender affordability assessment.

TruWorth does not assume two currencies are equivalent. When a product and financial profile use different currencies, financial-fit calculations should pause until you confirm the basis.

4. Wants You vs Real Buyer You

As your history grows, TruWorth can compare what you consider with what you actually buy and later report using. The aim is to highlight patterns such as whether you tend to browse for enjoyment but buy for utility, overestimate use in certain categories, or become more selective over time.

These patterns are dynamic. They are not psychological diagnoses or permanent personality labels.

5. TruWorth+ purchase vote

The premium vote is deliberately more decisive.Eligible subscribers may receive a BUY or DON’T BUY recommendation, together with confidence and the main factors that influenced it.

The vote can combine the core assessment, stress-test resilience, optional financial fit and relevant patterns from your TruWorth history. A quick in-shop vote based on a snapped product may have less information than a full assessment and should therefore be treated with correspondingly less confidence.

6. What the vote does not know

TruWorth may not know about upcoming bills, emergency needs, income instability, dependants, tax obligations, borrowing terms, medical needs, relationship circumstances or any other factor you have not entered. It also cannot predict whether a product will actually deliver the enjoyment or usefulness you expect.

7. Product identification

Snap & Assess prioritises structured identifiers such as barcodes and then uses text recognition or product catalogues. If confidence is poor, TruWorth should ask you to confirm or type the product rather than silently guess. Product identity, price and variant should always be checked before scoring.

8. You remain in control

A high score is not permission to spend and a low score is not a prohibition. TruWorth exists to expose assumptions, trade-offs and behavioural patterns so you can make a more deliberate decision.

For important financial decisions, debt, investments, regulated financial products or situations where affordability is uncertain, consider appropriate professional advice rather than relying on TruWorth.
